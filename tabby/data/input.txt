Once upon a time, in a quiet house at the end of a long lane, there lived a tabby cat named Tabby.
Tabby had orange stripes, green eyes, and a tail that curled like a question mark.
Every morning, Tabby woke up with the sun and stretched her paws on the warm windowsill.
She watched the birds in the garden and dreamed of chasing them through the tall grass.

The house was full of little wonders. There was a red ball of yarn under the sofa,
a cardboard box by the door, and a sunny spot on the rug that moved across the floor all day.
Tabby followed the sunny spot from the kitchen to the hallway, and from the hallway to the bed.
When the sun went down, she curled up on the soft blanket and purred herself to sleep.

One day, a small grey kitten arrived at the house. His name was Pip.
Pip was shy at first. He hid behind the curtains and peeked out with wide blue eyes.
Tabby walked over slowly, sniffed his nose, and gave him a gentle lick on the ear.
From that day on, Tabby and Pip were the best of friends.

Tabby taught Pip everything she knew. She showed him how to climb the bookshelf,
how to catch a feather on a string, and how to ask for breakfast with a polite meow.
Pip learned quickly. He pounced on the yarn, he tumbled in the box, and he chased his own tail.
At night they slept together in a warm little heap, one orange and one grey.

In the spring, the garden bloomed with yellow flowers and buzzing bees.
Tabby and Pip sat by the window and watched the rain fall on the leaves.
When the rain stopped, they ran outside and splashed through the puddles.
Their paws were muddy, their fur was wet, but they were happy.

In the summer, the days were long and lazy. Tabby napped in the shade of the old tree.
Pip climbed the fence and walked along the top like a tiny tightrope walker.
The neighbor's dog barked, and Pip jumped down and ran back to Tabby.
Tabby purred and told him that the dog was loud but kind, and that there was nothing to fear.

In the autumn, the leaves turned red and gold and fell in crunchy piles.
Tabby and Pip leaped into the piles and leaves flew everywhere.
They found acorns and chased them across the porch.
When the wind blew cold, they came inside and sat by the warm fire.

In the winter, snow covered the garden like a soft white blanket.
Tabby touched the snow with one paw, shook it off, and decided to stay inside.
Pip pressed his nose against the cold glass and watched the snowflakes dance.
Then they both curled up by the fire, and the house was quiet and warm.

Years went by, and Pip grew into a big strong cat.
But every night he still curled up next to Tabby, just like when he was small.
And every morning, they woke up with the sun, stretched their paws on the windowsill,
and watched the birds in the garden together.

The end.
